# Playbook: Ask for Advice, Not Feedback

**The counterintuitive, research-backed move.** When facing a hard, ambiguous, high-stakes call, our
ego says "work harder, go dark, handle it alone." Do the opposite.

## Why
- People who **ask for advice are rated as MORE competent** than those who don't — and the effect is
  **strongest on the hardest problems.**
- Advice **smears their fingerprints all over your decision.** It converts your boss from an *auditor*
  into a *co-owner* invested in your success.

## Word choice matters
Use **"advice"** or **"help"** — NOT "feedback."
- *Feedback* pulls them in vaguely, backward-looking, low investment.
- *Advice / help* is specific, forward-looking, and activates ownership.

## How to do it well (don't show up empty-handed)
- ❌ Weak: "What should I do about the Acme account?"
- ✅ Strong: "Here's what I'm recommending for Acme, and here's why. **What am I missing? What risks
  am I not seeing?**"

You still bring it as far as you can — you're pulling them in, not outsourcing the thinking.

## Guardrail (important)
**Only for genuinely high-stakes / ambiguous calls.** Asking for advice on things you should clearly
handle yourself **inverts the effect** — it recalibrates their estimate of you *down* and erodes trust.
Use it to pull them up, not to look helpless.

## AI use
"Here's my recommendation + reasoning on [decision]. Draft an advice-not-feedback ask for [boss] in
their preferred format. Flag if this is actually high-stakes enough to warrant it."
