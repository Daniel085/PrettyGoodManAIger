# Playbook: The 1:1 as a Decision Room (managing up)

**The shift:** a status-update 1:1 makes you a *reporter*; a decision-driven 1:1 makes you a
*leader*. Same 30 minutes, opposite posture — reactive→proactive, defense→offense.

- **Status 1:1:** "Here's what I did, here's what's next, see you next week." Boss leaves knowing
  what's handled — but *wondering if you're capable of more.*
- **Decision 1:1:** you walk in with the asks, the blockers only they can clear, the judgment calls
  you want their read on. You leave with **decisions actually made.**

## Why it's worth the discomfort
It feels riskier — you lay your judgment bare and make calls. That exposure is exactly how you beat
their expectations and become more valuable over time. (See `advice-not-feedback.md` for the asks.)

## Walk in with
1. **Asks you need answered** — concrete decisions you want out of the meeting.
2. **Blockers only they can clear** — escalations that need their leverage.
3. **Judgment calls** — "here's my read, here's my recommendation, where do you push back?"

## Prep prompt (the heart of the "second brain")
Feed: boss profile + last 1:1 notes + open commitments + this week's decisions/topics. Ask:
> "Prep my 1:1 with [boss]. Surface: open commitments either direction, the 2–3 decisions I should
> drive, blockers needing their leverage, and judgment calls to get their read on. Frame each in
> [boss]'s preferred format. Then pressure-test: what questions should I expect that I'm not
> answering, and where might [boss]'s typical fears creep back in?"

This is the prep you can schedule to run overnight, waiting for you when the day starts.
