# References & Lineage

The ideas in this kit are not invented here. This page credits the sources and maps each to where it
shows up in the system. See [PRINCIPLES.md](PRINCIPLES.md) for how each idea is operationalized.

## Primary inspiration — a public talk

**Dave Kline & Marsden Kline (MGMT Accelerator)** — a public talk/presentation on building a
"management second brain," promoting their "Build Your Management Second Brain" course on Maven.
This kit draws on ideas described in that talk; it does **not** use or reproduce any paid course
materials, which we have not seen. The seed concepts we borrowed: the "second brain as chief of
staff" idea; the three moves
**Decode → Deliver → Drive**; the boss **archetypes** (bottom-line, data-driven,
storytelling/relationships, detail-anxious/FOMO); "communicate what they need to hear, not what you
want to say"; the **30-second update**; **synthesis ≠ summary** and "quality of communication =
quality of thinking"; **advice-not-feedback**; the **1:1 as a decision room**; **playbooks** as the
"your brain" differentiator; and the practical guidance to run it in Claude / Cowork and keep
sensitive data out.
→ Throughout, especially the playbooks and both prep prompts.

## Management literature — managing up & influence

**John J. Gabarro & John P. Kotter — "Managing Your Boss."** *Harvard Business Review*, Jan 1980
(reprinted 2005 as an HBR Classic).
The foundational text on managing up. Source of: **mutual dependence** between two fallible people; the
"understand your boss" checklist (goals, pressures, strengths/weaknesses/blind spots, work style); the
matching "understand yourself" half (your needs and predisposition toward authority); and the
deliverables of a good boss relationship (keep them informed / be dependable & honest / use their time
selectively).
→ Boss dossier: **Mutual Dependence**, **Understand Yourself**, **Their World**. Principle **M2**.

**Allan R. Cohen & David L. Bradford — "Influence Without Authority"** (Wiley; 2nd ed. 2005).
Source of the **Law of Reciprocity** and the **"currencies"** model of exchange — inspiration, task,
position, relationship, and personal currencies — for gaining cooperation where you lack formal
authority.
→ Boss dossier: **Currencies They Value**. `prep-boss-1on1.md`. Principle **M4**.

**John Neffinger & Matthew Kohut — "Compelling People: The Hidden Qualities That Make Us Influential"**
(Hudson Street Press, 2013).
Source of the **strength × warmth** model of influence — that persuasion comes from balancing
projected competence/confidence with empathy/trust, and that most people over-index on one.
→ Boss dossier: **Understand Yourself → Strength × Warmth**. Principle **M5**.

### Further reading (not directly encoded, but adjacent)
- **Clay Scroggins — "How to Lead When You're Not in Charge"** — leading through influence without
  positional authority.
- **John C. Maxwell — "The 360° Leader"** — leading up, across, and down simultaneously; a reminder
  that the boss dossier should grow peer/stakeholder siblings.
- **Robert Cialdini et al. — "The Small BIG" / "Influence"** — the behavioral-science basis for small,
  high-leverage changes in how you ask and frame.

## Open-source mechanics

**huytieu/COG-second-brain** — <https://github.com/huytieu/COG-second-brain> (MIT License).
We do **not** fork it — its "people CRM" is deliberately a neutral, shareable contacts tracker, which
is philosophically opposed to a manager's private, candid coaching notes. But we borrow three of its
file *mechanics*, with thanks: the **two-layer record** (Compiled Truth + append-only Timeline), the
**citation discipline** (`[src | date | confidence]`), and **tiered enrichment** (stub → moderate →
full as you interact more).
→ All dossier templates; the capture prompt. Principles **P3, P4**.

## A note on attribution & accuracy

Quoted phrasing from the talk is transcribed from a live presentation and lightly cleaned; treat
quotes as faithful-in-substance rather than verbatim-to-the-word. We did not attend the paid course
or see its materials — only the public talk. The HBR and book citations are to
widely available published works — consult the originals for the full arguments, which are richer than
the one-line distillations used here.
